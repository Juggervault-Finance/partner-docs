An issuing partner is a licensed Issuing House or a licensed Finance House. Juggervault is the platform. The issuing partner is the licensed intermediary. Juggervault does not replace that licence.

The licensed pathway is set on the company. The access pathway is set on each deal. See [Access pathways](/guides/access-pathways).

## What the issuing partner provides

The person who registers the company must already have approved identity verification.

The company provides its legal details, selects at least one licensed pathway, and submits the documents for that pathway before company verification can be approved.

| Licensed pathway | Documents |
| --- | --- |
| Issuing House | SEC / Issuing House licence. Certificate of incorporation. Board resolution authorizing use of Juggervault for offer arrangement and investor registration. |
| Finance House | Finance House licence. Certificate of incorporation. Board resolution or appointment to manage issuing vehicles and use the platform for project financing. Trustee engagement evidence: a letter of engagement, appointment, or equivalent for a licensed security trustee. |
| Both | SEC / house licence. Certificate of incorporation. Board resolution or mandate. Trustee engagement evidence. |

An Issuing House may open an IPO, a corporate bond, commercial paper, a fixed deposit note, or an offering of another type that the issuer describes. Those programs do not place an issuing vehicle or a security trustee on the platform. The Issuing House keeps those particulars. A Finance House may open project financing. That program requires an issuing vehicle and a security trustee on the platform. A Fund Manager may open a mutual fund or a REIT. Those programs require a trustee on the platform and no issuing vehicle.

The company names the staff who may operate an offer.

| Access pathway | The issuing partner also provides |
| --- | --- |
| Pathway A | Its own investor portal and its own compliance operation. A consumer site address, when links in API responses should open that site. |
| Pathway B | Its brand, its staff, and its investor relationship. Partner staff, or an external approver by email, approve the deal. |
| Pathway C | The asset, a signed mandate, and approval through the external link. The issuer does not set the approval route. |

## What Juggervault provides

| Access pathway | Juggervault provides |
| --- | --- |
| Pathway A | Partner API read access, identity verification, issuance, settlement, reporting, and the asset registry. No hosted investor portal and no public listing. |
| Pathway B | The hosted application, a partner-branded section, identity verification, issuance, settlement, reporting, and the approval workflow. Example: `https://tokenization.juggervault.finance/partner/bglafrica`. |
| Pathway C | The public listing, investor onboarding, settlement, and reporting, under the operational mailbox `issuinghouse@juggervault.finance`. |

Company verification is reviewed on Juggervault before the company can prepare an offering. Billing for Pathway A and Pathway B is per transaction and per active deal, invoiced offline. Pathway C uses the standard Juggervault fees. See [Fees](/guides/fees).

A Pathway A API key can read records. It cannot create or change them. Distribution calls are described in [Broker partners](/guides/broker-partners).

## House Partner API

Issuing Houses and Finance Houses that operate deal lifecycle from their own systems use the **House Partner API** under `/api/partner/v1/house/*`. House keys are separate from broker keys.

### Provisioning

1. An administrator creates or selects the company on Juggervault.
2. An administrator creates a house partner record bound to that `companyId`.
3. An administrator enables Partner API, sets the webhook URL and signing secret, and issues a House key with selected house scopes.
4. The plaintext key is shown once. The key is already bound to that company. `POST /house/company` updates that company only. It does not create a new company.

### Deal lifecycle (House key)

| Step | Method and path | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read or update company | `GET` / `POST /house/company` | `house:company:write` or `house:offerings:read` (read) |
| Submit KYB documents | `POST /house/company/kyb` | `house:company:write` |
| Create or update offering | `POST` / `PUT /house/offerings` | `house:offerings:write` |
| Submit for approval | `POST /house/offerings/{offeringId}/submit` | `house:offerings:write` |
| Advance company approval step | `POST /house/offerings/{offeringId}/approve` | `house:approvals:write` |
| Open primary sale | `POST /house/offerings/{offeringId}/open-sale` | `house:sale:write` |
| List registrations | `GET /house/offerings/{offeringId}/registrations` | `house:registrations:read` |
| Admit investor (`whitelisted`) | `POST .../registrations/{investorId}/approve` | `house:registrations:write` |
| Settle intent (retired, returns `410`) | `POST .../intents/{intentId}/settle` | `house:settlement:write` |
| Investor report | `GET /house/offerings/{offeringId}/report` | `house:reports:read` |

`open-sale` saves primary-sale terms through the same services as the hosted application, opens registration acceptance when package approval is complete, and returns prepared listing calldata when on-chain listing is still required. It does not bypass factory-admin signatures.

Registration admit performs approve then complete registration so status becomes `whitelisted` and purchase can proceed.

Platform-admin approval steps remain Admin-only. They are not exposed on House routes.

Broker distribution endpoints under `/investors/*`, `/catalog`, and related paths remain broker-audience only. A House key receives `403` with `This key cannot access broker routes.` A broker key on `/house/*` receives `403` with `This key cannot access house routes.`
